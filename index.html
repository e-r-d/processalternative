Making a digital negative.

Contact processes print at the size of the negative, so most people now make negatives on an inkjet printer. The tool's "Save digital negative" button does the first three steps for you.

1. Convert and size

Convert the image to greyscale and size it to the final print. Contact printing has no enlargement: an 8×10 in print needs an 8×10 in negative.

2. Invert

Invert the image so shadows become clear film. Anthotypes are the exception: they bleach where light hits, so they print from a positive.

3. Flip

Flip the image horizontally if you print emulsion side down, so the ink side touches the coated paper. This keeps the print sharp.

4. Shape the curve

Each process has a different tonal range. Iron processes are short (cyanotype, about 1.5–2.0 log density range); salt and platinum are long (2.0–2.4); gum is very short per layer (about 1.0). Print a step tablet, read where tones block up or wash out, and build a correction curve.

5. Print on film

Print on inkjet transparency film made for negatives at the printer's highest quality, using black ink, or a colorized negative if your curve needs more UV density.

6. Test

Make a test strip, exposing sections for increasing times. The right time is the shortest one that gives a full shadow through clear film.
